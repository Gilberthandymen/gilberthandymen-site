Fall Home Checklist for Gilbert Homeowners

October 2026 · Seasonal tips · Gilbert & the East Valley

After a long Arizona summer, fall is when most of us finally want to be outside working on the house again. It’s also the best window of the year for a lot of small repairs: the heat is gone, the holidays aren’t here yet, and anything the sun beat up since May is easy to spot. Here’s the short list I’d run through around a typical East Valley home.

1. Smoke and CO detector batteries

The rest of the country gets a reminder when the clocks change. We don’t change clocks in Arizona, so a lot of detectors here go years on the same battery. Pick a day this fall, test every detector, and swap the batteries. If a unit is more than about 10 years old (the date is printed on the back), it’s time to replace it.

2. Flip your ceiling fans

Most ceiling fans have a small switch on the motor housing. In summer, the blades should push air down. On cooler nights, flip it so the fan runs slow and pulls air up, which moves warm air off the ceiling without a breeze on you. While you’re up there, wipe the dust off the blades and listen for wobble or clicking. A wobbly fan usually needs tightening or balancing, not replacing.

3. Outdoor and patio lights

Days are getting shorter, and summer heat is hard on outdoor fixtures, bulbs, and string lights. Walk the outside of the house after dark: garage lights, porch lights, patio strings. Replace dead bulbs, check for cracked fixtures or loose wiring, and get string lights re-hung securely before holiday decorating starts.

4. Exterior paint and trim touch-ups

The sun here is brutal on south- and west-facing trim, doors, and fascia. Look for peeling, chalky, or cracked paint. Fall temperatures are ideal for exterior painting because the paint can cure properly instead of baking on in 110-degree heat. Catching it now protects the wood underneath before winter rain.

5. Door seals and weatherstripping

Weatherstripping dries out and shrinks over summer. If you can see daylight around an exterior door or feel a draft on a cool morning, replace the seal or adjust the door. It keeps dust and bugs out and helps with heating bills on the few cold nights we get.

6. Look for small gaps and holes

Check eaves, vents, and where pipes and wires enter the house. Small gaps are an open door for pests, and in the East Valley that includes bees looking for a sheltered spot. Sealing an entry point now is a lot easier than dealing with a colony in a wall later. If you do hear buzzing, see our bee removal and repair service.

7. Faucets, filters, and the garbage disposal

Before family comes over for the holidays, fix that dripping faucet, change the fridge or under-sink water filter, and make sure the disposal is running right. A slow drip wastes water every hour of the day, and these are usually quick fixes.
